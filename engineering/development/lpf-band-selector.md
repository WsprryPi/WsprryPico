# LPF and band selector

Optional direct GPIO selection lets a member choose an external filter for each complete job. It is useful for unattended changes between declared frequency ranges; a fixed-frequency station can retain fixed/manual filtering without allocating a GPIO. The implementation reuses the existing pin validation, journal, management carriers and local RF authority. It adds no expander or relay framework. Software support does not establish a physical bank's switching time, cost or filter response.

## Settings and ownership

`hardware::FilterSettings` contains a policy, release and settling intervals, named physical selectors and named frequency ranges. Defaults are `fixed_manual`, 20 ms release, 20 ms settling and empty lists. Fixed/manual policy keeps every declared selector inactive and performs no automatic coverage admission. `required` refuses jobs when no single selector covers the complete job, including an empty mapping.

At most five selectors and ten ranges are accepted. Each selector has an integer ID from 1 through 5, a unique GP number, a name and explicit `active_high` polarity. IDs, selector names and pins cannot repeat. Names contain 1–16 ASCII letters, numbers, spaces, `/`, `_` or `-`, with no leading or trailing space. Range names are unique. Ranges refer to an existing selector ID and contain whole-Hz `low_hz` and `high_hz` from 1 through 1,000,000,000 inclusive. Low must not exceed high. Names are labels, not band lookup rules. Multiple ranges may share one selector. Ranges belonging to different selectors must not overlap, including their endpoints.

Eligible outputs are GP0–GP22 and GP26–GP28. Each selector owns its GP once, independently of the number of ranges referring to it. Allocation is exclusive against RF, button, amplifier enable, external indicator and both saved I²C pins. Firmware validates the whole model against the other roles before persistence and again during loading and adapter initialization. The older bare `PinPlan.lpf_gps` array remains operationally rejected: it cannot describe coverage, polarity or timing and grants no selector execution authority.

Five outputs suit a small five-position bank while preserving ordinary RF/button/amplifier pins. Release and settling each accept 0–250 whole milliseconds, separately. Their combined maximum is 500 ms; together with the maximum 1000 ms amplifier lead and local preparation slack this fits the existing two-second standalone preparation window. These are software bounds, not measured relay requirements. A 1536-byte settings limit and the existing 1984-byte aggregate state budget may reject a large combination of configuration, schedules and names before any write. Shorter names or fewer ranges can reduce the stored size.

## Complete-job admission

`rf::FilterCoverage` intersects the selectors covering every RF-on event. Both its requested nominal frequency and its programmed NCO frequency must fall within declared ranges for the same physical selector. Nominal endpoints are inclusive; programmed values must be strictly inside the whole-Hz endpoints, providing a conservative 1 nHz guard around rounded realization. Declare enough usable coverage for correction, modulation shifts and quantization. Several disjoint ranges on one selector can cover different events; separate selectors cannot be combined inside one job.

`StreamEngine` prepares the actual NCO plan and freezes the selector before ARM. `FrequencyPolicyEngine` applies the same coverage rule around every engine, including the inhibited simulator, using immutable boot settings. Native engine limits and amateur/experimental frequency permissions remain separate checks. WSPR tones, FSKCW/DFCW shifts, arbitrary accepted event frequencies and manual correction all enter this complete-job boundary. No GPIO commands or filter names are added to WTP/1.

## Local sequence and cleanup

Boot initializes each validated output inactive by loading its inactive latch before enabling output direction, then checking function, direction and level. Core 0 transfers the validated immutable plan to the existing RF worker. Inhibited firmware initializes outputs inactive and never hands them to its simulated engine.

For a required selection, the local sink clamps RF, confirms amplifier inactivity, deactivates all selectors, waits the release interval, activates the chosen selector and waits the settling interval. Only then can amplifier lead begin, followed by the scheduled RF start. Timing uses bounded local alarm stages; there are no network-dependent waits, sleeps, allocations, storage writes or logs in the timing path. ARM advertises and enforces the combined preparation through the existing `minimum_arm_lead_ns`. Insufficient time, late preparation, changed clock admission or failed GPIO verification refuses launch without moving the requested RF window.

Selection remains stable through the complete job, its keyed gaps and normal amplifier tail. Normal completion releases the selector after amplifier disable. STOP, ABORT, maintenance, reset, shutdown, missed launch and fault cleanup immediately clamp RF, attempt amplifier disable, attempt selector disable, then perform fallible DMA cleanup. Every attempt runs even if an earlier one fails. Armed/running lease expiry retains the existing finite-job terminal extension. Unconfirmed output cleanup retains inhibition and ownership; emergency recovery forces validated selector pins inactive before watchdog recovery. GPIO readback describes a commanded electrical state, not relay contacts or measured RF silence.

## Management and persistence

The current unreleased application and public-read contracts are extended in place. No additional historical contract or state version is introduced. `capabilities.filter_selector` is exactly `{"schema":"transmitter-filter-selector/1","path":"/api/v1/filter-selector"}`. Capabilities have an 8192-byte bounded response budget. The settings, readback and descriptor schemas are in `engineering/protocol/transmitter-filter-selector-*.schema.json`.

GET `/api/v1/filter-selector` returns exactly `schema`, member `target`, `saved`, `boot_active`, `pending_restart`, `selected_id`, `selection_known`, `admission_error`, `output_active`, `rf_active`, `storage_healthy` and `reserved`. `reserved` identifies other GP owners. `admission_error` is null or `filter_coverage_required`, the last preparation refusal. `selected_id` is null for inactive or unknown selection; `selection_known` distinguishes them. RF and selector observations travel together through one locked sink snapshot and worker report. They are observations at readback, not a physical interlock measurement.

PUT contains exactly `schema`, device/boot `target` and complete `settings`, with the current Config ETag in `If-Match`. The body limit is 1792 bytes. Changes require healthy storage, an idle unowned nonfaulted member and confirmed inactive output. All selector changes, including mapping or policy changes, require restart and latch output inhibition for the boot, including A/B/A saves. Saving never toggles GPIO or starts a job. Saved values, immutable boot values and the accepted job selection are distinct.

Settings use optional `filter_selector` in the existing `standalone-state/4` record, with no flash address or journal capacity change. Default records retain their existing encoding. Omitted settings reconstruct fixed/manual defaults; Config, station, progress, message and logging edits preserve the selector domain. Invalid or corrupt stored settings fail closed. Restore fixed/manual defaults with supporting firmware before loading an older image that cannot parse configured selectors; there is no automatic downgrade or fallback. Configuration reset clears selectors under the existing reset authority.

Pico browser, authenticated network/Consumer-Radio and USB management all reach the same handler. WsprryPi validates the descriptor, resource shape, carrier/profile, identity, boot, revision, limits and confirmed readback before treating a save as successful. Its selected-member resource is `/api/v1/member/filter-selector`; native Pi Band GPIO and frequency-suffix behavior remain separate. Unsupported peers refuse explicitly without retrying a different carrier. UI drafts survive reads, errors and context changes; an uncertain save requires readback before another explicit save.

## Wiring and operation

Use suitable external drivers with 3.3 V logic inputs and external bias that keeps the bank inactive during reset, boot ROM and loss of Pico power. Share the required signal ground. Never connect relay coils directly to GPIO. Software cannot establish a safe unpowered state. One output corresponds to one independently selected path; encoded address buses, latching relays and expanders are unsupported.

Use **LPF / band selector** under Pico **Station Control → Radio pins**, or the selected member's hardware controls in WsprryPi Fleet. Read saved selectors, name each output, choose its GP and polarity, then map measured usable frequency intervals to it. Set release and settling for the actual driver/bank, choose the required policy, save while idle, explicitly restart and read saved/active state again. Keep fixed/manual filtering when automatic selection is unnecessary. Declared coverage does not verify attenuation, insertion loss, power handling, emissions or suitability for an antenna.

## Validation and applicability

Registered `filter_selector`, `filter_selector_schema` and `filter_selector_browser` tests cover closed settings, pin ownership, shared selectors, complete-job nominal/programmed coverage, boot-frozen state, journal failure, identity/revision refusals, A/B/A inhibition and cleanup under multiple failures. Both production network and USB source-pair suites cover readback, changed limits, malformed documents and preservation through ordinary saves. The opt-in `operator_cleanup_browser.mjs --filter-ui-only` workflow exercises the shipped editor through the production HTTP/crypto fixture; its driver and output arguments are documented in the [development guide](README.md).

Recorded software acceptance uses candidate changes over Pico `e2fa85e55193e2ce051629ac1787f69600620666` plus documentation revision `d8eec8167c88279835d7d9b61c03055483f42593`, and Pi `e8d10e11c155cfbb4f441e39ae2e42f5f7e08915`, with MbedTLS `0bebf8b8c7f07abe3571ded48a11aa907a1ffb20`. These are edited candidate sources, not clean baseline-revision results. Normal and inhibited Pico 2 W images cross-link with the pinned SDK/toolchain and existing flash, stack, heap and USB audits. No selector bank, relay timing, filter response or additional GPIO/RF campaign is qualified by those checks. Physical applicability requires the actual driver, bank and assembled RF path; amplifier GPIO observations do not establish selector contact timing.
