# Si5351 planning and device control

The portable Si5351 library provides synthesis planning, exclusive resource validation, bounded register control, and raw-clock reference measurement calculations. It is not selected by the application RF factory and adds no advertised engine or browser control. The application continues to use direct GPIO/PIO generation. GPS/PPS integration is outside the current task.

## Planning

`src/rf/si5351_plan.*` constructs fixed-size plans for at most four distinct tones. The plan binds the complete synthesis configuration, manual correction, requested frequencies, quantization budget, achieved frequencies, and divider/register representations. Device configuration rebuilds and checks that plan before bus writes.

PLLA remains fixed across transmit tones. A selected loopback uses PLLB and a separate nominal 1 MHz divider plan unaffected by applied transmit correction. None uses an absent output selection; CLK0 is a real output. Positive correction means a fast source: the programmed transmit frequency is divided by `1 + correction_ppb / 1e9` once. Correction is bounded to the existing ±100,000 ppb range. These calculations establish no physical carrier accuracy.

`src/rf/si5351_job.*` compiles complete `rf-events/1` jobs into fixed storage of at most 4 KiB, with 512 event ends, four distinct tones, an immutable job digest, and the existing one-hour limit. Absolute event boundaries round independently to microseconds. The caller must supply a qualified minimum transition interval. Unsupported frequencies, excess tones, malformed timelines, and missing frequency-adjustment consent are refused without hardware access. This compiler does not execute a job or establish adapter timing.

The numerical implementation covers 25 MHz and 27 MHz references and CLK0–CLK2. It uses a nominal 899 MHz PLL, leaves margin below the VCO ceiling, and keeps the MultiSynth output at least 1 MHz before the R divider. Special divide-by-4/divide-by-6 plans and PLL-retuned modulation are refused. Low-frequency and upper-frequency admission follows the complete corrected plan, rather than a band label. Synthesis feasibility does not qualify any mode or transition.

## Registers and resource ownership

The supported register model is Si5351A 10-MSOP with standard address `0x60`. An ACK does not identify the silicon or establish module compatibility. Other packages, custom addressing, and substitutes require separate support. Passive crystals require an explicit 6, 8, or 10 pF load. Register 183 uses the documented reserved-bit encoding. External XA restores the documented 10 pF reset selection rather than retaining a previous crystal setting; it is never translated into CLKIN selection. The selected external module is the QRP Labs Rev. 5 board with its 25 MHz TCXO, installed according to its assembly manual without the supplied crystal and C3. Other external-XA arrangements require the datasheet's coupling, amplitude, and floating-XB requirements and independent qualification. Register settings do not prove that wiring or input levels meet those requirements.

`src/hardware/si5351_settings.*` validates one approved I²C pair, distinct transmit/reference outputs, optional loopback input, optional external gate, and existing GPIO owners. PWM counting requires an eligible B-channel input and reserves its entire slice independently of GPIO ownership. GP7 maps to slice 3 B; GP6/GP7 cannot simultaneously serve as the I²C pair. No physical wiring or gate presence follows from accepted allocation.

`Device` distinguishes unknown output state from register-disabled and register-enabled observations. Initialization masks outputs and powers down all three supported outputs before programming. Readback failure, NACK, and partial writes retain unknown state. PLLA and PLLB lock observations remain separate. No output can be enabled before the applicable lock observation. Quiescence attempts both mask and powerdown control. None of these observations measures physical RF inactivity.

`PicoBus` is an RF-owner adapter with no constructor side effects. It uses the approved hardware I²C controller at 400 kHz, recomputes the remaining absolute deadline between transfer phases, and caps individual transfer budgets at 5 ms. Failure closes the controller; recovery requires owner-controlled reopening. No clock output is enabled by opening the bus.

## Reference measurements

Reference windows contain extended edge counts, raw monotonic endpoints, capture uncertainty, and hardware-plan generation. The bounded 16-bit counter extension rejects service gaps that could hide multiple wraps using a hardware edge-rate bound, independently of the nominal input frequency. Coherent snapshots and endpoint brackets participate in the uncertainty calculation. Plan changes invalidate a window.

`PicoReferenceCapture` implements input-only PWM sampling with exclusive slice ownership, outward-rounded timestamp brackets, a conservative hardware edge-rate bound, and clock-tree-change invalidation. It requires frequent owner polling and rejects starvation. It is compiled but not instantiated by the application. The physical setup and service budgets remain unqualified.

Measurement-only policy produces a ratio and uncertainty. Trusted-local policy additionally requires an explicit nonzero oscillator uncertainty allowance and can calculate a local rate estimate. Neither supplies a UTC epoch or absolute RF calibration. The library does not apply the estimate to μChrony or the application clock. Freshness checks do not refresh observation age. Capture integration, clock-source arbitration, and runtime application remain unfinished.

## Validation boundary

The registered `si5351_plan`, `si5351_job`, `si5351_allocation`, `si5351_device`, `reference_measurement`, and `reference_counter` tests exercise arithmetic, complete timelines, refusal before writes, failed/partial control, lock separation, resource conflicts, rollover ambiguity, plan binding, and uncertainty. `pico_reference_capture` compiles the actual capture adapter against a behavioral SDK fixture that checks input direction through every GPIO handover, slice ownership, counting, starvation, and clock changes. These are deterministic software checks. The SDK adapters are compiled by the normal firmware build, but the application does not instantiate them.

The remaining production work is tracked in the [consolidated backlog](consolidated-backlog.md). It includes JobService/worker selection, execution and shutdown timing, capture integration, atomic persisted configuration, coordinated current control schemas and consumers, conditional browser controls, and physical qualification. The existing 100 µs completion acknowledgement bound is unchanged. A separately powered generator requires an explicit fault/reset shutdown design; a direct-GPIO clamp cannot stop it.

## Source provenance

This is original MIT implementation using mathematical/register facts from [Skyworks AN619 revision 0.8](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/SkyWorks/SL/documents/public/application-notes/AN619.pdf) and the [Si5351 revision 1.3 datasheet](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/SkyWorks/SL/documents/public/data-sheets/Si5351-B.pdf). The supported-module decision must also account for the manufacturer's [QRP Labs revision 5 assembly manual](https://qrp-labs.com/images/synth/synth_assembly5.pdf). The approved GPIO mapping follows the pinned Pico SDK and the [RP2350 datasheet](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2350/rp2350-datasheet.pdf). No manufacturer's example driver is incorporated.
