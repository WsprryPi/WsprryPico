# Si5351 engine and reference measurement

The application selects either direct GPIO/PIO generation or the Si5351 engine from the immutable accepted boot allocation. Si5351 controls, persistence, portable planning, local execution and reference measurement use the existing JobService and RF worker. Ordinary builds permit Si5351 transmission and reference-output activation only for the qualified QRP Labs Rev. 6 / 25 MHz TCXO profile. Other configurations remain engineering-gated. The explicit `WsprryPico-Si5351Engineering` target (or `WSPRRY_PICO_SI5351_ENGINEERING=ON` in a private build) enables physically unqualified execution for a separately verified conducted test setup. GPS/PPS ingestion and automatic RF calibration are unavailable.

## Planning

`src/rf/si5351_plan.*` constructs fixed-size plans for at most four distinct tones. The plan binds the complete synthesis configuration, manual correction, requested frequencies, quantization budget, achieved frequencies, and divider/register representations. Device configuration rebuilds and checks that plan before bus writes.

Below the VHF divider boundary, PLLA remains fixed across transmit tones. VHF plans use a common integer divide-by-six or divide-by-four MultiSynth and retune PLLA for each tone, without symbol-time resets. A selected loopback uses PLLB and a separate nominal 1 MHz divider plan unaffected by applied transmit correction. None uses an absent output selection; CLK0 is a real output. Positive correction means a fast source: the programmed transmit frequency is divided by `1 + correction_ppb / 1e9` once. Correction is bounded to the existing ±100,000 ppb range. These calculations establish no physical carrier accuracy.

`src/rf/si5351_job.*` compiles complete `rf-events/1` jobs into fixed storage of at most 4 KiB, with 512 event ends, four distinct tones, an immutable job digest, and the existing one-hour limit. Absolute event boundaries round independently to microseconds. The caller must supply a qualified minimum transition interval. Unsupported frequencies, excess tones, malformed timelines, and missing frequency-adjustment consent are refused without hardware access. The engine consumes this immutable plan locally, with a minimum 10 ms event interval. It admits WSPR, finite tone, QRSS, FSKCW and DFCW only when the complete plan meets these limits. This numerical admission does not qualify physical modulation or timing.

The numerical implementation covers 25 MHz and 27 MHz references and CLK0–CLK2. It uses a nominal 899 MHz PLL, leaves margin below the VCO ceiling, and keeps the MultiSynth output at least 1 MHz before the R divider. VHF plans use the special divide-by-four or divide-by-six paths within a 600–900 MHz VCO range. Every tone in a job must fit one common divider; incompatible tone sets are refused before writes. The numerical output range is 8 kHz–160 MHz. VHF tones share one feedback integer parameter and use a bounded family of large denominators: 983040–1048575 with a maximum span of 4095. A deterministic search considers at most 32768 candidates and retains the 0.001 Hz numerical error budget. Plans without a compatible family are refused before hardware preparation. Physical qualification is separate. Low-frequency and upper-frequency admission follows the complete corrected plan, rather than a band label. Synthesis feasibility does not qualify any mode or transition.

## Registers and resource ownership

Each job restores its complete initial transmit PLL and divider plan, including after a VHF job leaves PLLA on its final tone. Between jobs, initialization holds the transmit mask disabled and sets the new output mode with its driver powered down before completing the divider map and resetting PLLA. An established PLLB loopback is preserved. Compatible symbol retunes do not reset either PLL.

The supported register model is Si5351A 10-MSOP with standard address `0x60`. The capability identifier `si5351a_10_msop` names this register model, not an electronically identified silicon part. An ACK does not identify the silicon or establish module compatibility. The selected QRP Labs Rev. 6 module uses the vendor-supported MS5351M substitute with the same register model. An I²C response does not distinguish the two parts. Other packages, custom addressing, and modules require separate support. Passive crystals require an explicit 6, 8, or 10 pF load. Register 183 uses the documented reserved-bit encoding. External XA restores the documented 10 pF reset selection rather than retaining a previous crystal setting; it is never translated into CLKIN selection. The selected external module is the QRP Labs Rev. 6 board with its 25 MHz TCXO, installed according to its assembly manual without the supplied crystal and C3. Other external-XA arrangements require the datasheet's coupling, amplitude, and floating-XB requirements and independent qualification. Register settings do not prove that wiring or input levels meet those requirements.

`src/hardware/si5351_settings.*` validates one approved I²C pair, distinct transmit/reference outputs, optional loopback input, optional external gate, and existing GPIO owners. PWM counting requires an eligible B-channel input and reserves its entire slice independently of GPIO ownership. GP7 maps to slice 3 B; GP6/GP7 cannot simultaneously serve as the I²C pair. No physical wiring or gate presence follows from accepted allocation.

The immutable boot engine owns its execution storage. Large direct-waveform buffers are allocated only for a direct-engine boot, leaving that memory available to Si5351 job admission. Both selections retain the same authoritative WTP memory reserve.

`Device` distinguishes unknown output state from register-disabled and register-enabled observations. Initialization masks outputs and powers down all eight internal output drivers before programming, including the unused drivers required by the 10-MSOP register map. Only CLK0–CLK2 are exposed as supported outputs. Readback failure, NACK, and partial writes retain unknown state. PLLA and PLLB lock observations remain separate. No output can be enabled before the applicable lock observation. Quiescence attempts both mask and powerdown control. None of these observations measures physical RF inactivity.

`PicoBus` is an RF-owner adapter with no constructor side effects. It uses the approved hardware I²C controller at 100 kHz, recomputes the remaining absolute deadline between transfer phases, and caps individual transfer budgets at 5 ms. Failure closes the controller; recovery requires owner-controlled reopening. No clock output is enabled by opening the bus.

## Reference measurements

Reference windows contain extended edge counts, raw monotonic endpoints, capture uncertainty, and hardware-plan generation. The bounded 16-bit counter extension rejects service gaps that could hide multiple wraps using a hardware edge-rate bound, independently of the nominal input frequency. Coherent snapshots and endpoint brackets participate in the uncertainty calculation. Plan changes invalidate a window.

`PicoReferenceCapture` implements input-only PWM sampling with exclusive slice ownership, outward-rounded timestamp brackets, a conservative hardware edge-rate bound, and clock-tree-change invalidation. It requires frequent owner polling and rejects starvation. It is instantiated only for the selected Si5351 allocation. Physical observations remain scoped to the recorded profile and finite measurement windows; they do not establish absolute reference accuracy.

Polling within the same timer tick or across an interrupt-widened bracket does not publish a new endpoint. The next accepted endpoint includes all intervening edges and still enforces the original hardware wrap-ambiguity bound. Clock or generation changes and backward timestamps invalidate capture. Register-0 XA loss detection uses `LOS_XTAL` bit 3 for both passive crystals and external XA references; the Si5351C CLKIN detector is independent.

Measurement-only policy produces a ratio and uncertainty. Trusted-local policy additionally requires an explicit nonzero oscillator uncertainty allowance and can calculate a local rate estimate. Neither supplies a UTC epoch or absolute RF calibration. The application passes timestamped trusted-local rate observations to the existing μChrony engine, at most once per second, with their original sample time and expiry. This assists μChrony-derived UTC and accepted browser/phone time. Browser time uses the same measured rate directly in its existing UTC mapping, without another estimator. A fresh reference with lower uncertainty than the ordinary 50 ppm timer allowance changes only the mapping slope, with continuous UTC and preserved accumulated uncertainty, phone source age, authentication and expiry. Reference loss retains the learned center under conservative drift growth. Explicit policy removal returns browser UTC to the uncorrected slope and μChrony to its independent network-rate estimate, or nominal rate if none exists, without a time step. The one-hour browser source-age limit remains unchanged. UTC uncertainty is informational for first-party submissions; independent external WTP request limits retain their meaning. Other controller epoch observations retain their existing policy. A rate observation cannot establish UTC, refresh an NTP sample age, change raw hardware time, or correct RF. Independent rate disagreement blocks UTC admission across all epoch sources. Missing or stale capture retires freshness, retains the learned rate center with conservative ordinary holdover, and cannot clear a known disagreement. Explicit policy removal or a new independent agreeing observation is required. Freshness checks never refresh observation age.

## Validation boundary

The registered `si5351_plan`, `si5351_job`, `si5351_allocation`, `si5351_device`, `reference_measurement`, and `reference_counter` tests exercise arithmetic, complete timelines, refusal before writes, failed/partial control, lock separation, resource conflicts, rollover ambiguity, plan binding, and uncertainty. `pico_reference_capture` compiles the actual capture adapter against a behavioral SDK fixture that checks input direction through every GPIO handover, slice ownership, counting, starvation, and clock changes. Engine, monitor, settings, schema and shared browser tests also exercise complete execution, bounded shutdown, interrupted windows, current closed settings, refusal before persistence, None/CLK0 and restart latching. These are deterministic software checks. Both actual companion network and USB production pair suites are required for the intended sources.

The [conducted results](si5351-band-support.json) record 85 accepted mode/point cells and 51 expected-message WSPR decodes for the exact identified engineering image. This matrix does not qualify other module/reference/drive configurations or broad frequency intervals. No register readback or software test proves physical RF inactivity. A separately powered generator can continue through Pico reset or an I²C failure; an optional independently biased RF gate is a distinct resource, not an inferred feature of the module.

## Configuration and observations

RF Output offers Direct GPIO or Si5351. Selecting an engine changes only the browser draft. Si5351 requires an explicit approved SDA/SCL pair, fitted reference frequency/source and transmit output. Crystal load is visible only for passive crystals. Loopback is None or a distinct CLK0/1/2 with an eligible input GPIO. None releases capture resources and dependent trusted use. Reference use defaults to measurement only; trusted use requires a declared uncertainty. Drive is 2/4/6/8 mA and is independent of reported WSPR dBm. Manual correction is engine-specific and survives engine changes without transferring between oscillators.

The current Control/1 config accepts optional `si5351` settings. Engine activation uses one atomic `config/update` merge containing the complete `pins` and `si5351` allocation, checked together with separate filter resources before writes. Inactive saved Si5351 settings own no GPIOs. Hardware/reference changes latch restart and output inhibition, including A/B/A saves; the boot plan never changes in place. Station, network, STOP and existing journal size limits remain independent.

GET `/api/control/v1/si5351` exposes saved settings, boot-active settings, restart state, engineering gate and nullable runtime evidence. Communication means verified register control or unknown, not silicon identification. Transmit and reference register states are independent. Measurements report raw endpoints, counts, endpoint uncertainty, ratio, age, plan generation and reference-use policy. Samples older than 30 seconds are omitted. The age of the last reference-rate observation remains separately reported, including during capture loss. Clock-source status distinguishes selected, lower-quality, awaiting UTC, expired and disagreement. The same resource is available through authenticated Pi management over USB, engineering TLS and Consumer-Radio.

## Execution and electrical limits

All preparation, symbol-time I²C, output control and cleanup belong to the RF owner. Background measurement pauses before a job and interrupted windows are discarded. Finite reference windows can resume only when the engine is idle and TX registers are verified disabled, including after verified cleanup of a failed or missed job. The terminal job remains failed or missed; reference acquisition does not clear its ownership or restart it. Unknown TX state blocks acquisition. Permanent configuration or safety inhibition suppresses reference restart. PLLA stays fixed for MultiSynth retunes and is retuned for VHF integer-divider plans; PLLB supplies the independent loopback. No per-symbol output inhibition or PLL reset is introduced for continuous modes. Amplifier/filter lead, settle and tail intervals remain local and complete; failed control retains unknown output state and prevents terminal success.

Direct PIO/DMA retains its 100 µs autonomous hardware-acknowledgement limit. Si5351 uses an explicitly separate external-control completion path, bounded to 8 ms and capped independently by the existing advertised WTP output-disable timeout. Final output-off is commanded at the final event boundary; successful ACK remains unknown until register verification. JobService must confirm shutdown before terminal success. This is output-control latency, not permission to claim sample-accurate RF cutoff or measured timing. A single register write needs 27 I²C clocks (270 µs at 100 kHz); complete keyed retune/prime operations need several writes and readbacks. The 8 ms operation budget fits inside the admitted 10 ms minimum event interval. Transfer deadlines, missed-boundary refusal and unknown-state handling remain enforced.

The adapter uses 100 kHz and enables Pico internal pull-ups, matching the documented bench baseline. A faster bus or a wiring change is not a prerequisite. Electrical levels, rise time, transfer reliability, physical transition latency and failed-bus/reset containment still require measurement. An independent gate can provide a separate physical cutoff when explicitly wired and verified; none is inferred from accepted settings. Software ACK timestamps do not establish physical RF latency.

Watchdog-reset, stuck-SDA, and stuck-SCL conducted tests verified startup quiescence and honest fault ownership, unknown output state, replacement refusal, and controlled recovery. The carrier persisted during failed bus control, as reported; this is not an independent RF cutoff guarantee. Permanent wiring faults and power interruption are not covered by those observations. Ordinary firmware supports only the declared qualified profile; other profiles remain engineering-gated.

## Reference allowance and transmission gaps

The fitted validation module is the QRP Labs 25 MHz TCXO option. Its [product page](https://shop.qrp-labs.com/tcxo) describes measured temperature behavior within about ±0.25 ppm from approximately 4–65 °C; this is not a complete initial-tolerance, warmup, supply and aging specification. No automatic total-error value is inferred from that description. Unknown references retain measurement-only operation. Trusted use requires an allowance supported for the actual reference and operating conditions; ±500 ppb is an illustrative calculation, not a qualification requirement.

The [independent RAM-only fixture](../../tests/hardware/si5351-reference-truth/README.md) brackets CLK2 counts and raw timer time at receive-only QLG3 PPS edges. Its finite result and conditional truth-source allowance must remain distinct from production GPS ingestion and ordinary-image UTC launch accuracy. Recorded-input replay uses the actual measurement, NTP adapter, browser arbiter and UTC mapping. It can exercise withheld observations over WSPR-shaped intervals without pretending that the replay itself transmitted RF.

Acquisition remains paused during accepted jobs. The PWM extension requires owner polling within less than one possible wrap at the hardware edge-rate bound, while a bounded I²C transition can occupy several milliseconds. Extending acquisition through those intervals would require a different capture/interrupt design and new RF interference/timing qualification. Retaining the learned center avoids adding those unqualified resources to the RF owner.

The current conservative Pico drift allowance remains 50,000 ppb. It describes unobserved Pico timer drift, independently of the TCXO and capture errors; it is not replaced by the TCXO temperature figure. Browser UTC and μChrony add reference/estimate error to that allowance and preserve previously accumulated phase uncertainty. For an illustrative 5 ppm reference plus 1 ppm capture allowance, the combined 56 ppm growth contributes about 6.19 ms over a 110.592-second WSPR frame, 6.72 ms over 120 seconds and 201.6 ms over one hour, in addition to the original epoch error. The learned rate center is retained through capture loss; it does not abruptly revert to nominal rate at 30 seconds. A tighter Pico wander model and wider environmental or one-hour physical qualification remain separate evidence gates. UTC uncertainty is displayed rather than imposing a first-party transmission cutoff.
